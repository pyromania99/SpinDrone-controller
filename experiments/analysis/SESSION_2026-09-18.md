# Session results, 2026-09-17/18 — envelope, baselines, and two refuted theories

Everything measured in one long session. Written because none of it existed outside a chat
transcript. **Nothing here is a re-reading of old logs unless it says so** — all flights are
new, on the firmware described in §0.

Confidence is marked: **[n=5]** and similar give the repeat count; unmarked cells are
**n = 1**, and this project has one documented case (level-2 circle, 20 ms) where an n = 1
cell turned out to be 1-in-4 bimodal. Treat single cells as provisional.

---

## 0. What changed in the code before any of these flights

| change | where | effect |
|---|---|---|
| observer runs on every arm | `examples/utils/eigen_controller.py`, PX4 `EigenController.cpp` | **α is now measured with the seat OFF**, which it never was before |
| `Seat::measure()` split out | PX4 `Seat.hpp/.cpp` | one formula for α, used by both the law and the logger |
| `seat_stepped` field | PX4 `McControllerStatus.msg` | separates "the law stepped" from "α was measurable" |
| circular rolling mean | `experiments/tools/plot_run.py` | α is an angle; the arithmetic mean read uniform noise as **zero misalignment** |
| attitude-error panel | `plot_run.py`, `plot_level1.py` | tilt ≠ attitude error on a trajectory (see §6) |
| level-1 trajectory figure | `plot_level1.py` | `fig_trajectory` was simply never called |

**The measurement is provably passive.** All three level-1 arms were re-flown and came back
**bit-for-bit identical** (max |diff| = 0 on position, torque, angular velocity, motor
thrusts, θ_s). PX4 is not bit-reproducible, so there the check is statistical: seat-off
arms reproduced their crash times to within 0.2 s.

## 1. The headline results

### 1a. Hover rescue at level 3 — the best-established result in the tree **[n=5]**

r ≈ 29.5, `rotor_tau` 0.02 (39.5 ms total loop delay), PX4's own estimator in the loop.

| controller | outcome |
|---|---|
| eigen, seat OFF | crash 12.7 s |
| **eigen, seat ADAPTIVE** | **flies the full 46 s, 5 of 5 runs** |
| stock cascaded PD (`MC_CTRL_ALG=1`) | crash 14.3 s, inverted (179°) by t = 16 s |

θ_s across the five runs: **−0.4731 ± 0.0009 — 0.19 % spread.** Attitude error 0.25–0.37°.

That 0.19 % is the tell. The same measurement on the level-2 circle scattered −0.505,
−0.848, −0.561, −0.535 across four runs (40 %). **Where the seat works, θ_s is repeatable;
where it fails, θ_s is not.** That is a candidate diagnostic and it is untested as one.

### 1b. The coning case — largest measured seat effect

r ≈ 20, `rotor_tau` 0.02, level 3. **Both arms survive**, so this is not a survival binary:

| | attitude error | \|p,q\| |
|---|---|---|
| seat OFF | **13.40°** (coning limit cycle) | 1.54 |
| seat ON | **0.28°** | 0.088 |

**47.7× on a continuous metric.** Survival alone would have scored these equal.

### 1c. Stock cascaded PD does not fly any of it

| condition (level 3) | PD outcome | first-window tilt (eigen for comparison) |
|---|---|---|
| nolag, r ≈ 29.5 | crash 56.7 s, 8 crossings, spin ran to 46 rad/s | 8.94° (eigen 0.20°) |
| r ≈ 20 + 20 ms pole | crash 24.3 s, inverted by t = 30 s | 3.57° (eigen+seat 0.22°) |
| r ≈ 29.5 + 20 ms pole | crash 14.3 s, inverted by t = 16 s | 7.41° (eigen 0.19°) |

PD never held attitude at any condition — it is a weak baseline, not a competing design.
**Caveat:** PD ran on defaults. "Stock PD as shipped cannot do this" is supported; "no
cascaded PD could" is not.

### 2f. THE LEVEL-3 TRAJECTORY RESCUE — r ≈ 29.5, no rotor pole

Same circle, same spin, same rung; only `MC_SEAT_MODE` differs:

| seat | outcome | following distance | att err | θ_s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | **crash 35.4 s**, 4 crossings | **32.62 m off path** | 39.27° | 0 |
| **adaptive** | **FLIES 52.2 s**, 2 crossings | **5.04 m** | **4.48°** | −0.5516 |

The seat-ON arm's 5.04 m / 4.48° are the same numbers it posts at r = 25.9 where everything
flies, so it is tracking normally at a spin that grounds the unassisted vehicle.

**Why this cell separates and the 20 ms cells do not:** with no rotor pole the actuator only
*rotates*, and rotation is what the seat cancels. A 20 ms pole adds amplitude **droop**
(0.86 at this spin) which the seat cannot undo — hence both arms dying at 20 ms at every
speed from 2 to 5 m/s (§2d). The seat supplies phase, not authority.

`analysis/circle_l3_envelope/`. **n = 1 on both arms** — repeat before quoting.

## 3. Two theories proposed and refuted, in this session

Recorded because both looked convincing before they were tested.

### 3a. Off-axis gain — REFUTED

`control_doc` row 7 notes a delay is all-pass only on the imaginary axis; at the closed-loop
pole Re(s) = −`MC_EIG_WN` it has gain e^{|a|τ}. Predicted knee at wn ≈ 17. Swept wn at
L3 hover, r ≈ 29.5, 20 ms, seat OFF:

| MC_EIG_WN | \|a\|τ | pole gain | outcome | **r at departure** | mean \|τ\|/max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 34.18 | 1.350 | 3.86× | crash 12.7 s | 27.91 | 0.939 |
| 20 | 0.790 | 2.20× | crash 12.4 s | 27.75 | 0.923 |
| 17 | 0.672 | 1.96× | crash 10.7 s | 27.82 | 0.938 |
| 12 | 0.474 | 1.61× | crash 11.2 s | 27.00 | 0.918 |
| 8 | 0.316 | 1.37× | crash 10.9 s | 26.60 | 0.686 |

**Departure spin 27.42 ± 0.52 rad/s (1.9 %) across a 4.3× bandwidth sweep.** The off-axis
gain fell 2.4× and the boundary did not move. Refuted.

### 3b. Total loop delay as the ordering variable — REFUTED

23.5 ms flies at r = 28; 20 ms fails at r = 29.5. Less delay, barely more spin, opposite
outcome.

### 3c. What the sweep found instead — actuator saturation **[measured, not inferred]**

| run | mean \|τ_xy\|/max | peak | allocator clipped | motors pinned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wn=34 seat OFF — crashes | **0.939** | 1.414 | 79.0 % | **87.4 %** |
| wn=17 seat OFF — crashes | **0.938** | 1.414 | 27.0 % | **87.9 %** |
| wn=34 seat ON — flies | **0.040** | 0.142 | 4.3 % | 1.2 % |
| wn=34 OFF nolag — flies | **0.036** | 0.455 | 2.9 % | 1.1 % |

**23× separation in torque demand between flying and failing arms.** This explains why
every linear prediction came out ~2× optimistic: the failures happen saturated, where
superposition does not hold and "phase" is not defined.

From the allocation matrix, with Σu pinned at hover:

> **τ_xy^max = k_f · b · (2U − (S − 2U)),  U = min(S/2, ω_max²),  S = mg/k_f**

= **1.91 N·m** at b = 0.13 m, against `MC_EIG_TRQ_MAX` = 3.843 N·m which is only the
*normaliser*. **So the real saturation threshold is ≈ 0.50 of normalised torque, not 1.0** —
and the failing arms sit at 0.92–0.94 while the flying arms sit at 0.04.

Same equation gives the spin: `rot_dir` is [−1,−1,−1,−1], so drag moments **add**,
τ_z = k_m·S = **1.03 N·m** at hover thrust. Spin is the third row of the allocator, which
is why r_ss is set by k_m and mg alone (r ≈ k_m × 4.9e7, verified 20/25/29.4).

**Still not derived:** why demand grows with r until it crosses the ceiling. Rotor-pole
attenuation gives 1.14× at r = 27.4 and misdirection by ψ gives ~2×; neither reaches 23×.

### 3d. Droop orders the hover data — UNTESTED HYPOTHESIS

Phase does not separate fly from fail (L3 nolag hover flies at 0.575 rad; L1 20 ms hover
fails at 0.533 rad). Droop = 1/√(1+(rT)²) does, within hover, across all three rungs:

| droop | cases | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0000 | L1 & L3 hover, no pole | FLIES |
| 0.9937 | L3 hover, 4 ms, r = 28 | FLIES |
| 0.8937 | L1 hover, 20 ms, r = 25 | **FLIES** |
| 0.8613 | L1, L2, L3 hover, 20 ms, r = 29.5 | **FAILS (all three rungs)** |

Consistent with §3c — droop means less torque delivered per unit commanded. **But the
circle contradicts it** (L1 flies at droop 0.888, L3 fails at 0.970), and every cell is
n = 1. The discriminating test is a droop-matched pair: same droop, very different phase.

## 4. α, now that it is measured on both arms

| condition | circular-mean α, seat OFF → ON | resultant R |
|---|---|---|
| L2 circle, nolag | **+0.35 → −0.13** | 0.76 → 0.80 |
| L3 circle, nolag | **+0.45 → −0.13** | 0.82 → 0.82 |
| L2 hover, nolag | +1.04 → **−2.94** | 0.92 → **0.37** |
| L3 hover, nolag | +1.08 → **−2.84** | 0.84 → **0.37** |

**On the circle α behaves as designed** — a standing misalignment driven toward zero, at
R ≈ 0.8 so the direction is real. **In hover it does not shrink**; it moves to near ±π and
concentrates half as well. That is the starved-channel case `Seat.adapt` describes, but
nothing here tests whether that explains it.

**α is not a residual.** Both implementations build the observer's commanded vector from
the torque *before* the seat rotates it, so α on a seat-ON arm is the misalignment being
corrected, not what is left after.

### The seat as a delay estimator

τ_eff = (θ_s − atan(rT))/r on the well-excited circle:

| arm | r | θ_s | τ_eff |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2 circle, nolag | 20.70 | 0.4008 | **19.4 ms** |
| L3 circle, nolag | 25.87 | 0.5033 | **19.5 ms** |

Two rungs, two spin rates, agreeing to **0.1 ms**. This is the measured value of PX4's
transport delay used throughout this document.

## 6. Two measurement errors corrected

**Tilt is not attitude error on a trajectory.** The circle commands ~21° of lean:

| | commanded | flown | true error |
|---|---|---|---|
| L3 circle | 21.11° | 19.52° | **3.78°** |
| L2 hover | 0.06° | 0.12° | 0.17° |
| L1 hover | 0.375° | 0.299° | 0.471° |

Reading tilt as error overstated it **5×** on every trajectory figure.

**α needs a circular mean.** Post-crash the level-1 FIXED arm's α is uniform around the
circle (R = 0.004); the arithmetic mean is ≈ 0, so the plot drew a confident flat line at
*zero misalignment* for 110 s of ground contact.

## 7. Hardware notes

- **Level 2 does not exist on hardware.** `MC_CTRL_GT=1` subscribes to
  `vehicle_attitude_groundtruth` / `vehicle_local_position_groundtruth`, whose only
  publisher in the PX4 tree is `ReplayEkf2.cpp`. On a real vehicle nothing publishes them,
  `.update()` never returns true, and the 50 ms `MC_CTRL_WD_MS` watchdog trips into the
  fallback cascade. Everything that ports is level-3-style.
- **The gps.py radians/degrees fix is simulator-only.** Real receivers emit degE7 and PX4's
  driver has always handled it; there is no reprojection step to get wrong.
- **The EKF2 output-predictor fix must travel.** PX4 core, commit `f7953526b0`, bites above
  r_crit = (π/2)/τ = 15.7 rad/s. **Not upstreamed** — it exists only in the local clone.
- **`--max-lateral-accel` defaults to 0.0 = disabled** at level 1 and every level-1
  trajectory result depends on it. Omitting it turned a clean 65 s flight into a crash 13 s
  in and produced a wholly spurious "the seat is harmful on circles" (see
  `../trajectory_level1_circle_seat/RUN_LOG.md`).
- **You are flying at 93 % of actuator ceiling in the failure cases.** On hardware that
  margin is worse — battery sag, motor derating, wind. Write the torque budget down before
  a first flight.

## 8. What is NOT established

- **The level-3 trajectory rescue (§2f) is n = 1 on both arms.** It is the only trajectory
  cell where the arms separate. At r = 25 they cross the lag boundary together, and with a
  20 ms pole neither survives at any speed — so the rescue exists in a narrow corner
  (high spin, no rotor pole) and its reproducibility is untested.
- **No validated analytic limit.** The linear cascade predicts r_crit ≈ 48; measured 27.4.
  Two candidate mechanisms were refuted here. The saturation budget is measured but its
  r-dependence is underived.
- **Most cells are n = 1.**
- **PD is untuned.**
- **The hover-vs-circle α contradiction (§4) is unexplained.**
